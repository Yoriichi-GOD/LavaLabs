What these data mean

NASA POWER supplies MERRA-2 model/reanalysis values at 0.5° latitude × 0.625° longitude native meteorology resolution. A coordinate selects a grid cell. This is not a rain gauge, satellite image or city-wide average.

How change is measured

Temperature uses day-weighted annual means. Precipitation converts monthly mean daily rates (mm/day) to annual totals using actual month lengths. Years with missing months are excluded; a broken annual sequence receives no significance result.

The rate is an ordinary least-squares slope per decade. “Fitted change” is slope × elapsed years; it is not the difference between two observed endpoints.

Statistical assumptions, correction and limits

At least 20 complete, consecutive annual values are required. Uncertainty uses Newey–West HAC standard errors with a Bartlett kernel, lag 3, a finite-sample correction n/(n−2), and a Student t reference with n−2 degrees of freedom. This is an approximate, exploratory inference under a linear-trend model with weakly dependent residuals.

Holm adjustment covers all 36 fixed comparisons in this demonstration: six coordinates × two variables × three periods. The displayed 95% slope interval is marginal, before that correction. Statistical evidence uses the adjusted p-value at α = 0.05. A nonsignificant result means insufficient evidence under this test; it does not establish no change.

Lag choices 1 and 5 are provided as sensitivity checks in the evidence download. Long-memory dependence, model bias, structural breaks and spatial representativeness are unresolved. The test does not identify a cause, make a forecast or quantify practical importance. Regional differences shown here are descriptive; their difference has not been tested.
